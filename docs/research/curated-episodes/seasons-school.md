# Seasons and school-event episode research

## Executive summary

This report identifies **60 viable episode candidates** across the seven assigned themes: 8 for `summer-vacation`, 7 for `winter-snow-day`, 9 for `spring`, 9 for `autumn-fall`, 10 for `back-to-school`, 8 for `school-festival`, and 9 for `prom-school-dance`. Every theme therefore clears the seven-candidate research target and has at least seven distinct series available for a five-episode final selection.

The strongest pools are `summer-vacation`, `back-to-school`, `school-festival`, and `prom-school-dance`: their episodes make the theme an actual plot engine rather than incidental scenery. `spring` and `autumn-fall` also clear the target, but their best first-party evidence skews toward children's animation. That is a feature of the available evidence, not a title-only inference. Anime is particularly well represented in `summer-vacation`, `spring`, `back-to-school`, and `school-festival`; Western animation is represented in every seasonal pool and in `prom-school-dance`.

TMDB mapping is resolved for 39 candidates and intentionally left pending for 21. The largest concentration of pending mappings is in short-form and preschool animation, where Apple TV commonly exposes compilation or absolute-style episode numbers that do not align with TMDB season order. Those conflicts are recorded rather than silently normalized.

## Methodology

- Candidates were accepted only when a distributor, network, studio, or official streaming page described plot action that directly depends on the theme. A matching title by itself was not enough.
- Apple TV is used here as first-party distributor metadata. Netflix Tudum and Netflix title pages are used for Netflix originals. TMDB links establish **series identity and candidate mapping only**; they are not used as sole evidence that an episode fits a theme.
- “Original air date” is reported only when the source appears to identify the original release. If an Apple storefront exposes a later localization/distribution date, the field is marked unresolved or the conflict is stated.
- `Mapping pending` means that no sufficiently reliable public TMDB series URL was established during this pass. It does not mean the series is absent from TMDB.
- `High` means the episode identity and thematic plot relevance are directly supported and there is no material unresolved numbering issue. `Medium` means the thematic fit is supported but packaging, numbering, or date metadata still needs reconciliation before manifest entry.
- Format labels distinguish live action, Western animation, and anime so that final curation can preserve medium diversity.

## Source and normalization notes

1. **Apple TV dates are locale-sensitive.** Apple serves network/studio/distributor metadata, but a storefront can show a dubbed, licensed, or catalog-release date rather than the original broadcast. This is visible in *Cardcaptor Sakura*, *Moribito*, *Buffy*, and several children's series. Such values are explicitly excluded from the “original air date” field above.
2. **Compilation numbering is the principal metadata risk.** Nickelodeon, PBS, Disney, and preschool catalogs frequently package two short stories as one storefront episode or expose an absolute segment number. The affected candidates include *Phineas and Ferb*, *Hey Arnold!*, *Arthur*, *SpongeBob SquarePants*, *Peppa Pig*, *Caillou*, *Charlie and Lola*, and *Mickey's Farm*.
3. **Anime broadcast order can differ from chronological or distributor order.** The clearest case is “Live Alive”: Apple labels it *The Melancholy of Haruhi Suzumiya* S1E26 while [TMDB lists it within S1 as E12](https://www.themoviedb.org/tv/42511/season/1/episode/6). The report records both and leaves the manifest key unresolved.
4. **TMDB IDs are identity metadata, not editorial evidence.** A TMDB series URL is linked only when the public URL was sufficiently established. Missing IDs are marked `Mapping pending`, never inferred from a similar title.
5. **Secondary evidence was used only once as plot support.** The *Simpsons* snow-day row uses an official Disney+ page for episode identity and a stable Plex guide for the inaccessible synopsis. It should be replaced with a directly accessible Disney/Fox synopsis if one becomes available before final curation.
